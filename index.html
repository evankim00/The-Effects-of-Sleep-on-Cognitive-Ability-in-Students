A lapse is any response of 500 ms or slower on a 10-minute attention test4. Sleepiness is the Karolinska scale, from 1 (extremely alert) to 9 (fighting sleep).

Missed words are only part of the cost. Teens restricted to 5 hours a night took in new material less well3 and forgot more of what they did learn: 26% more after 30 minutes and 65% more after six weeks2.

Where the numbers come from

The model is fitted to measured data from teenagers. Beyond that, it follows the shape found in adults, and it stops where the research stops. D is sleep debt in hours, n is nights so far, and h is hours slept per night.

MeasureCalculationBasis
Sleep debt D = n × (8 − h) Hours short of 8, the low end of the 8–10 hours recommended for teens5. No weekend catch-up is assumed.
Lapses 2 + 0.68 × D, up to D = 23.8
2 + 16.2 × (D ÷ 23.8)0.67 above that
After night 14: L14 + 14 × s × (1 − e−(n − 14) ÷ 14)		 Teens at 5 hours in bed slept 3.4 hours less than controls each night. Their lapses rose from about 2 to 18.2 in 7 nights, and a straight line fits every measured night within 1.4 lapses1. Beyond that, the curve bends with the 0.67 exponent fitted over 14 nights in adults6. No study goes past 14 nights, so after that the estimate keeps rising at the night-14 rate s and levels off over another 14 nights. That last step is our assumption.
Sleepiness 4.7 + 2.3 × √((8 − h) ÷ 3.4) × (1 − e−n ÷ 2.5) Rested teens rated about 4.7–4.8. Restricted teens rose to 6.9 by night 7 and leveled off1. The curve matches each measured night within 0.8.
Words missed Lapses ÷ 95 A 10-minute test shows about 95 prompts, 2–10 seconds apart. The lecture assumes each lapse costs the same share of words. This step is an illustration, not a measurement.
  • Adults kept to 4–6 hours in bed for 14 days performed as badly as after one to two full nights without sleep, yet rated themselves only slightly sleepy6.
  • No controlled study has restricted teens for a whole semester. Real students also sleep in on weekends, drink caffeine and differ from one another.
